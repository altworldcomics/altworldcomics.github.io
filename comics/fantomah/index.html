Who Is Fantomah?

Fantomah is a supernatural jungle heroine from the earliest years of American comic books. She first appeared in Jungle Comics #2 in 1940, in a feature created by writer-artist Fletcher Hanks under the name Barclay Flagg.

Her original adventures combine jungle pulp, fantasy, horror, bizarre transformations, and supernatural punishment. She protects the jungle, but she has never fit neatly into the mold of a conventional superhero.

Publication History

1940 — The Debut

Fantomah debuted in Jungle Comics #2 in February 1940. The earliest stories established her as a mysterious supernatural protector whose adventures could move far beyond ordinary jungle fiction.

The Original Legend

The Golden Age stories embraced the anything-can-happen energy of early comics. Ivory hunters, scientists, monsters, strange civilizations, and supernatural enemies could all enter Fantomah's jungle.

1942 — Daughter of the Pharaohs

The feature changed substantially during its original run. Fantomah was reworked as an ancient Egyptian princess and the series moved away from the surreal supernatural avenger of the earliest Fletcher Hanks stories.

1944 — End of the Golden Age Run

Her final Golden Age adventure appeared in Jungle Comics #51 in 1944. The original run ended, but Fantomah would later be rediscovered as one of the more unusual figures from early American comics.

Why Fantomah Matters

Fantomah predates many better-known costumed heroines and comes from a period when the language of the American comic book was still being invented. Fletcher Hanks's stories are visually and narratively distinctive even beside other Golden Age comics.

Her historical importance, strange imagination, and relative obscurity make Fantomah a natural fit for AltWorld Comics' Remastered Classics: forgotten legends preserved, contextualized, and introduced to new readers.

The AltWorld Remastered Project

Remastered Classics does not exist to replace the historical comics. The goal is to preserve their identity and public-domain roots while presenting them in a format that is easier for modern digital readers to enter.

Our editions combine restoration and presentation work with new editorial material, historical context, and a consistent AltWorld Comics identity. Original creator credits and the distinction between historical material and modern presentation remain visible throughout the project.
